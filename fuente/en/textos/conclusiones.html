Conclusions

SO1. Identifying and characterising types of architectural journey

On the basis of the literature, a classification into five types of journey has been developed, characterised by their historical context, geography, architecture of interest, motivations and forms of record:

  • The classical or Enlightenment journey is oriented towards the direct study of ancient and Renaissance architecture, through analytical observation, measurement and drawing.
  • The romantic journey extends destinations and references towards the picturesque and what was considered exotic, incorporating a subjective gaze expressed in diaries and drawings attentive to the surroundings.
  • The modern journey combines the study of monuments with an interest in other architectural traditions, seeking principles that might contribute to the development of a language of one’s own.
  • The vernacular journey focuses on the ways of building and dwelling of cultures other than the traveller’s, with particular attention to their spatial and constructive resources.
  • The contemporary journey diversifies its references and forms of interpretation, incorporating both the reading of the city and architecture of the present and the perceptual experience of spaces.

The classification starts from Gonçalves Diez’s proposal and develops it by distinguishing five types, incorporating the vernacular journey as a category of its own and broadening the contemporary journey. The cases studied show that these types make it possible to order and compare different attitudes towards architecture, but they are not closed historical stages: their features can coexist in a single journey and reappear in later periods.

SO2. Selecting the case studies

Five journeys have been selected according to four criteria: typological representativeness, educational relevance, availability and quality of sources, and the architect’s historiographical relevance. The cases are John Soane’s journey through Italy and Malta (1778–1780), as a classical journey; Charles Garnier’s through Spain (1868), as romantic; Le Corbusier’s journey to the East (1911), as modern; Jørn Utzon’s through China (1958), as vernacular; and Tadao Ando’s to Europe, returning through Africa and Asia (1965), as contemporary.

The selection makes it possible to explore different ways of travelling and different moments in a professional career. However, the criteria do not carry the same weight in every case. Garnier stands out for his romantic representativeness and the precision of his diary, although the educational relevance of his journey could not be confirmed; Ando provides a particularly significant experience for interpreting the contemporary journey, despite the scarcity of accessible documentation. These differences show that the representativeness of a type of journey and its initiatory character are questions that must be assessed separately.

SO3. Gathering and selecting the information

Historical, spatial and chronological information on the five journeys has been gathered, together with written and graphic documentation of various kinds. For Soane, Garnier and Le Corbusier, sketchbooks, diaries, drawings and correspondence allow the routes to be reconstructed with greater precision. For Utzon, Chiu’s research and the visual records make it possible to approximate the sequence of the journey, though not to fix its dates exactly. For Ando, the reconstruction depends mainly on retrospective testimonies and a limited body of accessible graphic material.

The result shows that the nature of the sources determines both the reconstruction of the itinerary and the interpretation of the experience. The five journeys therefore cannot be analysed with an equivalent degree of certainty: documented data must be distinguished from estimates and hypotheses, and documentary gaps must not be read as an absence of experiences or influences.

SO4. Reconstructing and organising the itineraries in a GIS

The information gathered has been organised in a Geographic Information System through records of places, arrival and departure dates, sequences and, where available, documentary links. From this base, itinerary maps and chronological-geographical axes have been produced that make it possible to visualise movements, prolonged stays and returns to the same place.

The reconstruction reveals different ways of organising the journey, from Soane’s concentration on Rome and southern Italy to Garnier’s railway circuit through Spain or Ando’s intercontinental route. The GIS makes them easier to compare and provides an expandable base for future research. Its scope nevertheless depends on the information entered: representing an estimated date does not turn it into an established fact, and the length of a stay cannot by itself measure its educational importance.

SO5. Analysing the relationships between the journeys and later production

The study has identified relationships of different kinds between the travel experiences and the later architecture of their protagonists. For Soane, the classical and Palladian references stand out, together with the professional contacts made during his stay in Italy. For Le Corbusier, the records of the journey and the literature make it possible to relate the architecture visited to spatial and compositional devices in later works. For Utzon, the explicit reference to the Yungang caves is accompanied by historiographical hypotheses about the incorporation of constructive and spatial principles from Chinese architecture. For Ando, retrospective testimonies highlight the learning derived from the experience of space, light and the uses of architecture. For Garnier, the sources consulted do not confirm any specific impact of the journey to Spain.

These results indicate that what is learned from a journey can show itself through formal references, spatial criteria, constructive solutions or professional relationships. The cases reveal a diversity of ways of reworking what was seen, but not a linear replacement of formal influences by experiential ones. Moreover, the relationships identified have different degrees of documentary support, which must be preserved when formulating conclusions.

SO6. Comparing the cases and the evolution of the initiatory journey

The comparison shows a broadening of destinations and of the architecture considered worthy of study, as well as a diversification of motivations and means of record. The measured drawing, the diary, the sketch and the photograph express different ways of observing and working through what was seen. At the same time, shared references persist: the addition of new destinations does not eliminate interest in ancient monuments or in the routes associated with the classical journey.

The cases also make it possible to refine the concept of the initiatory journey. The architect’s age, or the mere fact of travelling, is not enough to establish it; one must assess the intention behind the journey, its position in the career and the signs of later transformation. Garnier is a borderline case: his experience is representative of the romantic journey, but the documentation consulted does not confirm the educational threshold required by the definition adopted.

Kolb’s theory helps to interpret the relationships between direct experience, reflection and later practice. However, the sources do not allow the four phases of the cycle to be verified consistently, nor do they support the conclusion that the lack of an identifiable architectural trace implies an interruption of learning.

With regard to the general objective

The thesis makes it possible to recognise transformations and continuities in architectural travel from the Grand Tour to the second half of the twentieth century. The five cases show that direct contact with architecture could contribute in diverse ways to the education and later practice of their protagonists. Its possible initiatory character must be established for each career and in the light of the available evidence. The proposed classification and the GIS reconstruction offer a framework for studying these relationships, whose scope can be tested and extended by adding new journeys.

Limitations

The thesis is based on five case studies, selected to explore the proposed types of journey. This selection makes it possible to compare experiences from different contexts, but a single case per type is not enough to draw general conclusions about each category or about the evolution of architectural travel as a whole. Moreover, the cases correspond to established architects, all of them men, whose careers have received considerable historiographical attention. Other, less documented experiences of travel and education are therefore left out.

The availability and precision of the sources are uneven. For Soane, Garnier and Le Corbusier, sketchbooks, diaries and correspondence allow the itineraries to be reconstructed in great detail. For Utzon, the general sequence of the route is known, but many of the dates are estimated; for Ando, the reconstruction is partial and depends mainly on retrospective testimonies. These differences limit the comparison of durations and of the temporal distribution of the stays. Representing them in the GIS makes the journeys easier to read, but does not resolve the uncertainties of the underlying documentation.

Access to sources has been conditioned by access to the archives. Part of the documentation has been known through publications by researchers who were able to examine it in person, while some holdings remain undigitised or are not available online. In Ando’s case there is also the impossibility of consulting the catalogue Tadao Ando: Endeavors and the linguistic difficulties of the Japanese-language documentation, which required recourse to machine translation.

Identifying influences on later architecture also has limits. The architects’ own testimonies, historiographical interpretations and similarities between works provide evidence of different nature and solidity. It is not always possible to isolate the impact of a particular journey from that of readings, earlier journeys, collaborations or other experiences. For this reason, a formal similarity does not in itself prove an influence, just as the lack of documentary evidence does not allow one to conclude that it did not exist.

Future lines of research

A first line of continuation would be to increase the number and diversity of case studies. Adding several journeys of each type would make it possible to test the proposed classification criteria, identify overlaps between categories and assess whether the trends observed hold in other professional careers. Likewise, including women architects, figures who have received less attention from historiography and travellers from diverse cultural contexts would make it possible to examine experiences and conditions of access to travel that are not represented in the present selection.

A second line would deepen the study of the cases with the largest documentary gaps. For Utzon and Ando, consulting new archival holdings and specialist publications could help refine both the itineraries and their chronology. For Garnier, analysing the project documentation and the decisions taken after 1868 would make it possible to verify possible relationships between the journey to Spain and his later architectural production. This research would also help to distinguish which references were acquired during each journey and which reached the architect by other routes.

Third, the study of journeys from the late twentieth and twenty-first centuries would extend the time frame of the research. It would be particularly relevant to analyse how digital media, remote access to images and documentary sources, and contemporary forms of mobility affect the preparation, recording and later working-through of architectural experience.

Finally, the GIS database offers various possibilities for development. On the one hand, the linking of drawings, photographs, correspondence and sketchbook pages to the stays they correspond to could be completed. On the other, specific fields could be added to record the provenance of each piece of data, its degree of spatial and temporal precision and whether it is documented, estimated or hypothetical. These improvements would facilitate the critical review of the reconstructions and make it possible to compare the journeys according to their different degrees of uncertainty.
